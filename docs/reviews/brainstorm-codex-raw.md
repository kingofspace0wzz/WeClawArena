Overall: this currently reads like 2 papers plus a taxonomy appendix. The strongest version is `runtime + benchmark`, with the framework demoted to an evaluation lens. The weakest current instinct is trying to make the five-dimension framework itself a headline contribution. Reviewers will read that as ontology inflation unless it unlocks a result AA cannot express.

1. The 3-contribution structure is overscoped for a main-track paper. You already have runtime novelty, benchmark construction, new metrics, new attacks, and an intervention ablation. That is enough. Adding a “Social Auditability Framework” as a third co-equal contribution makes the story diffuse and invites “jack of all trades” rejection. If you cut anything, cut the framework as a standalone contribution. Keep it as the formal scaffold for the benchmark/runtime, not as a separate claim.

2. The framework is only novel if the novelty is operational, not taxonomic. “Action Recoverability but messages are first-class,” “Lifecycle Coverage but with social phases,” and “Responsibility Attribution but with DAGs” all sound like sensible adaptations, not deep novelty. The sharpest claim is: auditability in social-agent settings requires executable attribution over propagation/inference chains under partial observability, and your runtime makes that measurable online. If you cannot show concrete failure cases where vanilla AA says two systems are equivalent but your runtime-derived social auditability separates them meaningfully, reviewers will call it a re-skin.

3. The 250-scenario design is plausible operationally, but statistically messy for the claims you seem to want. `250 x 3 seeds x 2 privacy levels x 2 audit conditions x 4 models ≈ 12k` runs is not the main problem. The problem is that 15 scenarios per `(attack family, category)` cell is thin if you want per-cell claims, and 3 seeds mostly probe stochasticity, not distributional coverage. You risk pseudoreplication: many runs, not much independent evidence. Also the hard-verification stratum is only 40 scenarios, which is too small if you want it to carry trust in your scoring pipeline. The safer move is fewer factors, stronger replication, and a hierarchical analysis that treats scenario as the unit, not run.

4. The scoring formulas are most vulnerable exactly where you already suspect. `Recoverability` via a “ground-truth shadow log” is only defensible if GT is mechanically generated or independently annotated with agreement stats; otherwise it is just agreement with your preferred latent decomposition. `Responsibility Attribution` via DAG F1 has the same issue, plus edge-level brittleness: many alternative DAGs can explain the same violation. Those two will get hit hardest. `Evidence Integrity` weights `0.25/0.5/0.75/1.0` also look arbitrary unless tied to a threat model or a cost model. `Phase Coverage` feels especially weak: reviewers may ask why correctly tagging “discovery/negotiation/repair” is auditability rather than just good metadata. `Policy Checkability` is also at risk of circularity if decidability depends on SEM state inferred from the same logs being scored.

5. `audit-off` vs `audit-on` does not cleanly isolate the contribution. Passive checks are fine, but once `audit-on` rewrites messages, you are changing the agent’s observation stream, task difficulty, and possibly token budget. That couples detection quality, intervention policy quality, and model adaptation effects. The “hard reject contaminates behavior” argument is reasonable, but rewrite contaminates it too. You need at least three conditions: no-audit instrumentation baseline, passive detect-only, and enforce-rewrite. Otherwise reviewers can say you measured the effect of a sanitizer, not auditability.

6. Missing pieces:
- Threat model for compromised auditor/gateway. Right now the runtime is a trusted oracle.
- Side channels: retrieval, tool outputs, summaries, metadata leakage, memory compression artifacts.
- Simple baselines: prompt-only defense ladder, rule-based DLP sanitizer, centralized post-hoc trace auditor, standard guardrail middleware.
- Validation of rewrite quality: does sanitized output preserve task semantics?
- Annotation protocol and cost for shadow logs / attribution DAGs.
- Venue-fit clarity. Is this a systems paper, benchmark paper, or safety-eval paper? Right now it is all three.
- Scope honesty. `1 agent per user` is a reasonable simplification, but it weakens the “agentic social networks” claim. It is really “social interaction among user-serving agents,” not the full team-of-agents problem.

7. Reviewer-2 objection: your central auditability metrics rely on author-defined latent ground truth that is neither naturally observable nor clearly unique, so the paper risks measuring conformity to your ontology rather than real auditability. Combined with the runtime+framework+benchmark bundling, that makes the empirical claims hard to trust even if the engineering is good.

If I were forcing this into one paper, I would make the claim much narrower: “a runtime-backed benchmark for measuring and reducing socially propagated privacy/integrity failures in persistent agent interactions,” with the framework serving that claim rather than competing with it.
